Project overview

One lending app for six products, run by executives, handed to the customer at the moments that need them

FinVista is the assisted lending app of one of India's largest NBFCs (client anonymized under NDA): 25 million+ customers, a loan book above Rs 30,000 crore, loans originated in 500+ cities. I led UX across all six products, with the two-wheeler flow as the proving ground.

TL;DR

Paper-heavy origination became an 11-step native Android journey run by sales, call centre and store staff, with designed handoff moments where the customer signs, consents and verifies in person. Six products on one 100+ component system. Processing ~60% faster, agent training down ~40%.

Who runs it

Three roles drive this app. None of them is the borrower.

Sales Centre Executives

Run loans over phone and app, a dozen applications at once, and own the compliance. Speed is the job.

Store Managers

Own the dealer portfolio, the pipeline and the escalations. They read the app more than they type into it.

The customer

Not a user. A participant, for the eight or so moments only they can complete: live photo, consents, OTP, uploads, e-signature.

The problem

One app, two personas, six products, and a gap from flagship phones to entry-level Android on patchy coverage

One scalable Android app, reusable across six products, holding up on every device and network in India.

The old flow was paper, agents and fragments. The new one had to be fast enough for twenty applications a day, instantly readable the second the phone turns around to face the customer, solid where the coverage is patchy, and RBI-compliant end to end.

Non-negotiables

The three rules the design had to keep.

  • Progressive Disclosure. One category of fields at a time. The load never spikes.
  • Trust at Every Step. Visible security cues and a plain reason for every field.
  • Two Readers, One Screen. Dense and fast for the executive; stripped back and plain at handoff.

Four decisions

What the design actually rests on.

  • No-Scroll Screen. One screen, one job. It mattered most for agents running 15 to 20 applications a day.
  • Dual Progress. Journey-level percentage plus step-level indicator. Orientation without overwhelm.
  • Assistive Onboarding. Every step opens with plain-language help, so the executive never translates the screen out loud.
  • Pre-verified Data. Existing customers get pre-filled fields with checkmarks and one-tap confirm.

In the store

Three rounds that changed the handoff

No formal lab. I shared Figma prototypes with real users, watched, and refined. These are the three rounds that changed the design.

01

Internal stakeholders

Scrollable multi-action screens overwhelmed everyone on wireframes. I pivoted to one screen, one action.

02

Sales executives

Six executives on mid-fi. Nobody could find the way back to edit a completed step, so edit icons became persistent.

03

Customers at handoff

Customers signed without reading. I rewrote every handoff screen to one plain question plus what happens next.

Honestly

What worked, and what I would change.

What went well

  • One screen, one action killed form fatigue for executives running back-to-back applications
  • Progressive disclosure made the 11-step journey "not feel long"
  • The early design system let later product journeys assemble in days

What I would do differently

  • Bring sales executives in on round one. Their deepest insight only surfaced in round two
  • Test on low-end Android devices sooner
  • Design error states from the start, not at the end
