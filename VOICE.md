# VOICE.md — how the site should sound

Read this before writing or editing any copy on khanfaraz.in. It applies to
case studies, page copy, captions, button labels, alt text, meta descriptions,
everything.

The problem this exists to fix: people read the site and say it sounds
AI-written. They are not wrong, and the reasons are specific and fixable.
They are listed below.

---

## 1. The voice, in one line

Write the way Faraz talks: short, direct, a bit clipped, no decoration. Say the
thing, then say why, then stop.

What that sounds like, from his own messages:

> "no, nothing is working. scrap and re-do"
> "park this for now."
> "we will keep the old case study but the visual design will be from the new one, the structure as well"
> "make them dormant or hide them for now. The company is not operational anymore."
> "I thought to take it at 30 because it might be my first gig, plus they will not make this info public"

And from copy he wrote himself, on the patterns page:

> "Go ahead, click things."

That is the register. Not a version of it. That.

**Rules that fall out of it**

- Lead with the verb. "Remove the filter" beats "The filter was removed".
- Ask, then reason, flat. No build-up, no reveal.
- Start sentences with **so**, **also**, **plus**, **but**, **and**. He does.
- Contractions are fine. Use them.
- "I" for decisions, "we" for the work the team did. Never "one" or "you can see that".
- If a sentence would sound strange said out loud to a colleague, it is wrong.

## 2. The six tells, with real examples from this site

These are measured from the current copy, not guessed. Tell 7 was added
after the first rewrite, because the rewrite introduced it.

### Tell 1: the "not X, but Y" flip

The loudest one. It appears dozens of times.

> "Amount, EMI, tenure and interest rate together, with the total cost stated rather than implied."
> "built for reading rather than entry."
> "Schedule and content on the same canvas instead of a wizard."
> "as options to pick from rather than a decision already made."

Nobody talks like this repeatedly. **Fix:** drop the second half, or split it.

- Before: "with the total cost stated rather than implied"
- After: "the total cost is on the screen. No arithmetic."

Allowed **once or twice per case study**, when the contrast is the actual point.
Not as a default sentence shape.

### Tell 2: everything arrives in threes

54 plain "X, Y and Z" triads across four case studies. Real speech gives you two
things, or four, or a list that trails off.

- Before: "visibility, graduated control, and an audit trail"
- After: "visibility and graduated control. The audit trail came later."

### Tell 3: every sentence is the same length

Measured across 1,088 sentences: mean 13.7 words, and **79% of them sit between
8 and 30 words**. Only 2% run past 30. That flatness is the giveaway. Human
writing lurches.

**Fix:** put a three-word sentence next to a forty-word one. Deliberately.

- Before: "The first wireframes put several actions on one scrolling screen. Stakeholders and engineers both lost the thread."
- After: "The first wireframes put four actions on one scrolling screen and everybody, stakeholders and engineers both, lost the thread within about a minute. It did not work."

### Tell 4: abstract nouns doing the work

> "the load-bearing trust mechanism"
> "the colleague-interrupt as the true competitor"
> "cognitive load collapsed to zero"

Nominalisations read as machine-generated because they compress an action into a
thing. **Fix:** put the verb back.

- Before: "Answer-provenance is the load-bearing trust mechanism throughout."
- After: "Every answer shows where it came from. That is the whole reason anyone trusts it."

### Tell 5: too tidy

Balanced clauses, matched rhythms, perfect parallel structure.

> "The raiser designs, prices and launches. The buyer arrives and orders."

**Fix:** break the symmetry. Let one side be longer or messier than the other.

### Tell 6: no mess anywhere

Not one aside. No "this took three goes". No small irrelevant detail. The single
most human sentence currently on the site is in the Jack of All Threads
blueprint:

> "I walked one real order end to end and wrote down every step, including the
> ones that happened in a WhatsApp group rather than in software."

That WhatsApp detail is what does it. It is specific, slightly off-topic, and no
model would have invented it.

**Fix:** every section gets at least one concrete, unglamorous detail. Where it
stalled. What got thrown away. The thing somebody said in the room.

### Tell 7: writing that performs

Added 2026-09-26 after the Aurora pass. This is the opposite failure to the
stiff ones above, and just as bad: copy that is trying to land a line.

Faraz, on reading his own rewritten case study:

> "i want nothing that sounds dramatic and rhymes, i want it to be just normal
> and natural. dont want to let people think what it means, just have to be direct"

The shapes to watch for:

- **Parallel with a twist.** "By the last round nobody was arguing about
  layouts. We were arguing about words."
- **Setup then knock-down.** "Three clicks to one looks like the win. It was not."
- **Alliteration.** "Useful for QA. Useless for anyone running campaigns."
- **Repetition for effect.** "It would have been a document nobody opened."
- **Metaphor.** "Shipped as the old thing wearing new paint."
- **Emphatic adverbs.** "That is what *actually* stopped the drift."

All of these make the reader stop and work out the point. The point should
already be on the page.

**Fix:** say it once, plainly, and move on.

- Before: "By the last round nobody was arguing about layouts. We were arguing about words."
- After: "By the last round the only feedback left was about wording and labels."

- Before: "Three clicks to one looks like the win. It was not. The win was that a marketer could see every journey's status the moment they opened the tool."
- After: "The click count went from three to one. The bigger saving was that a marketer could see the status of every journey as soon as they opened the tool."

**The same rule covers punchiness.** After Tell 7 was fixed, the copy was still
reading as written rather than said, because the fixes used a different set of
performance moves:

- **Fragments for emphasis.** "Three clicks before any actual work." "Names show in full."
- **Inversions.** "Engineers built it for engineers." "Underneath is a Figma kit."
- **Staccato.** Three short sentences in a row. "Customers kept asking for more control. So I could not delete anything. It had to fold away instead."
- **Colourful verbs.** "Chopped the ends off", "brought the year back".
- **Trailing emphasis.** "and nothing else", "either".

Plain means **complete sentences joined by ordinary conjunctions**. Subject, verb,
object. "so", "but", "and", "on top of that". Boring on purpose.

- Before: "Engineers built it for engineers. To start a campaign you left the dashboard, waited for another page to load, then went hunting for a button. Three clicks before any actual work."
- After: "The product was built by engineers with no designer involved. To start a campaign you had to leave the dashboard, wait for a separate page to load, and then find the create button on that page. That was three clicks before you did any work."

A good test: if a sentence would get a small nod of appreciation, cut it.
Nobody nods at how someone described a quarter filter.

## 3. Banned outright

Never ship these:

- **Em-dashes.** Existing rule. Use a comma, a full stop or brackets.
- **"It's worth noting", "that said", "at the end of the day", "ultimately".**
- **leverage, seamless, robust, crucial, delve, landscape, realm, testament,
  underscore, elevate, navigate (figurative), unlock, empower, transform
  (as a verb for design work), journey (unless it is literally the product's
  journey feature).**
- **"This isn't just X, it's Y."** Worst offender in the family.
- **Self-deprecation and integrity commentary.** No "I won't invent numbers",
  no "this is just a concept", no "I am being honest". Own the work. This was
  decided 2026-09-26 and applies to every case study.
- **Rhetorical questions** as section openers.
- **Sentences that exist to introduce the next sentence.**

## 4. Things that are fine and should stay

- Real numbers with a stated basis.
- Plain technical nouns. "Penny-drop verification" is not jargon, it is the name
  of the thing.
- Strong opinions. "The long scroll lost" is better than "the long scroll was
  found to be less effective".
- Short fragments as emphasis. "It did not work."

---

## 5. How to check

Read it out loud. If you run out of breath, or you would not say it to a
colleague over coffee, rewrite it.

Then check the mechanical ones:
- Count "rather than" and "instead of". More than two per case study is too many.
- Count "X, Y and Z" triads. Same limit.
- Look at sentence lengths in a paragraph. If they are all in the teens, break one.

Do **not** use an AI detector as the test. They flag clean human writing
routinely and clear machine writing sometimes. The target is "sounds like
Faraz", not "beats a tool".
